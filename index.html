Show Day Guide

Whether you're grooming for your first show or your fiftieth, here's a plain-English guide to your rabbit's key features and what actually happens on show day — pulled straight from the handbook's show-preparation and judging chapters.

Know your rabbit

  1. Nose — clean, with no discharge.
  2. Mouth / teeth — front teeth should meet evenly and not be overgrown.
  3. Eyes — bright, clear and open, no discharge or cloudiness.
  4. Ears — upright, clean, no scabs or mites.
  5. Fur / coat — dense, clean and glossy, no bald patches.
  6. Tail — straight and clean underneath.
  7. Private area — anus, and penis or vulva (depending on sex) — checked for cleanliness.
  8. Front paws — clean, standing square, toenails trimmed and not overgrown.
  9. Hind legs — straight, strong bone, even stance, toenails trimmed and not overgrown.

Before the show

  • Trim toenails about two weeks ahead, never the same day — freshly cut nails are sharper than long ones.
  • Brush out loose hair with a damp cloth or chamois so it doesn't shed on the show table.
  • Check ears for canker, and the nose and front paws for any signs of snuffles.
  • Confirm your rabbit sits correctly for its breed's posture, and that its ears sit the way the standard expects.

The night before

  • Some exhibitors rub a little cornflour or mielie meal into the coat to lift out dirt, then brush it all out completely — any leftover powder makes the coat look dull.
  • Pack your show box: pellets, a water bowl, a small towel or cloth, nail clippers, and a little cabbage or carrot.
  • Make sure your travel carrier keeps your rabbit dry even if it urinates on the way, and skip newspaper bedding under white rabbits — wet ink can stain the coat.

On show day

  • You're seen by the judge one at a time, not as a group at a shared judging table.
  • The day runs in three stages, in this order: a written knowledge test first, then washing and preparing your rabbit, then showmanship in front of the judge.
  • Do all your washing and prep before your turn — once you're called in for showmanship, there's no more time to fix anything.

During showmanship

  • You bring your rabbit to the judge and talk them through why it's ready for the show — condition, coat, and general health.
  • Explain what a judge actually checks: type and posture, coat, ears and eyes, toenails and paws, and overall health — show that you know what they're looking for, don't just hand the rabbit over.
  • It's a conversation with the judge, one exhibitor at a time — not a silent inspection while rabbits sit together at a table.

What the judge is really scoring

Showmanship isn't only about reciting facts — it's judged the same way real stockmanship competitions work, across a few things a judge is quietly watching the whole time you're in front of them, not just when they ask you a question.

First, how you handle your rabbit: gently and confidently, without force or rushing, and clearly aware of when it's getting stressed. Second, how well you actually control it — where you stand, how smoothly you move it into position, and how you keep both of you safe around tables, gates, or pens. Third, whether you can read its body language: noticing when it's about to react and keeping it calm, rather than letting a situation get away from you. And finally, whether you genuinely know your stuff — if the judge asks a question about feeding, health, or breeding, can you actually answer it, not just repeat a rehearsed line.

That's really what this whole quiz is building toward: the written test proves you know the facts, and showmanship proves you can actually use them — calmly, in front of a judge, with a real rabbit in your hands.

10 rabbit facts you won't find in the handbook

  • A rabbit's ears can rotate almost 270°, independently of each other, to pinpoint a sound without turning its head.
  • Because its eyes sit on the sides of its head, a rabbit can see almost all the way around its body — except for one small blind spot right in front of its nose.
  • A healthy rabbit's heart beats between 130 and 325 times a minute — up to three times faster than a resting human heart.
  • Rabbits physically cannot vomit, which is one reason a careful, gradual diet matters so much.
  • Rabbits produce special soft droppings called caecotrophs and eat them directly, giving their body a second chance to absorb nutrients.
  • A rabbit's front teeth can grow up to 5mm in a single week — about the width of two grains of rice.
  • Rabbits mark their territory by rubbing a scent gland under their chin on objects, a behaviour called "chinning."
  • A healthy adult rabbit can jump over a metre from a standing start — roughly waist-high on an adult person.
  • Rabbits thump their hind legs hard on the ground to warn other rabbits nearby that danger might be close.
  • Every domestic rabbit breed alive today descends from wild rabbits native to the Iberian Peninsula — modern-day Spain and Portugal.

About Rabbit Wise

This quiz was built from an official Youth Show rabbit handbook (guide) so that young exhibitors can practise their knowledge in a fun, low-pressure way before facing a judge's questions on the show bench.

There are three divisions, matched to the Youth Show age groups: Junior Junior, Junior and Senior. Each division's questions are worded for that age group specifically — simpler for younger exhibitors, and going into full technical depth for Seniors.

Where the content comes from

Questions are drawn from all eight chapters of the handbook: what a rabbit is, housing and sanitation, food and nutrition, breeding, health and disease, breeds, show preparation, and the show itself — including the practical judging checklist real judges use.

A note on the pictures

Breed-identification questions use real photos of each breed rather than illustrations.
